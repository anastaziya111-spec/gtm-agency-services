Business context

Northstar Ops

A fictional B2B software provider that helps multi-site manufacturers standardise operational workflows across plants. The company has an established product and wants to enter Germany, Austria and Switzerland.

Strategic objective

Prove a repeatable entry motion

Identify the best-fit subsegment, validate a buyer problem, sharpen the value proposition and build a channel mix that creates qualified sales conversations.

Key assumption to test

Operational inconsistency is costly

Multi-site operators may have fragmented processes and limited visibility between locations. Discovery must establish whether this is urgent, funded and owned by a reachable buyer.

Decision rule

Evidence before scale

Do not scale paid acquisition or broad outbound until the target segment, message, qualification signals and first-call conversion criteria have been tested.

Demand signals to validate

Expansion and complexity

New facilities, acquisitions, new ERP / MES programmes, rising quality costs and a push to standardise across sites.

Research output

Market choice memo

Prioritised subsegments, buying triggers, competitive alternatives, access constraints and a list of assumptions requiring primary research.

Ideal customer profile

Multi-site manufacturers with repeatable operations and a visible standardisation mandate.

Illustrative qualification boundaries: multiple operating locations, a central operations function, an existing core system landscape and a project owner with cross-site responsibility.

Economic buyer

COO / VP Operations

Concern: execution consistency, margin leakage and cross-site visibility. Needs a defensible business case and a clear path to measurable operational change.

Champion

Head of Operational Excellence

Concern: process adherence, continuous improvement and adoption. Needs practical workflow examples and a low-friction pilot path.

Technical approver

CIO / IT Director

Concern: integration, security, data access and support burden. Needs architecture clarity, implementation scope and integration requirements.

Financial reviewer

CFO / Finance Director

Concern: business case, total cost and payback assumptions. Needs cost baselines, measurable operational levers and staged investment.

Buyer roles and qualification criteria are fictional examples. Real ICP work would validate these roles through customer interviews, win/loss evidence and account-level research.
Positioning statement

For multi-site manufacturers that struggle to keep operational processes consistent across locations, Northstar Ops provides a shared workflow layer that makes execution visible and repeatable without replacing core plant systems.

Illustrative positioning hypothesis. It should be tested against buyer language and the actual product capability set.

Value pillar 01

Visibility

Give central operations a comparable view of process execution across locations.

Value pillar 02

Standardisation

Turn local workarounds into documented workflows and a consistent operating method.

Value pillar 03

Integration

Connect existing systems and operational teams instead of forcing a wholesale replacement.

Proof required

Operational evidence

Before publishing claims, gather measured examples for cycle time, process adherence, quality or administrative effort.

Messaging must map to product evidence. No performance claim or customer proof is implied by this sample statement.

Channel principle

Match each channel to the buyer's information needs and the sales team's capacity.

Use narrow channels to validate the offer first. Scale only after there is evidence of segment fit, message resonance and qualification quality.

ChannelRole in the motionInitial useValidation signal
Founder-led outboundDirect access to named accounts and discoverySmall, researched account cohortPositive replies and qualified discovery
Partner / integrator referralsBorrow trust and reach relevant operations leadersMap ecosystem partners and referral logicWarm introductions with clear context

Days 01-30 · Decide

Research and validate

Market and competitor review, buyer interviews, ICP boundaries, problem validation, current funnel baseline and first positioning hypothesis.

Gate: the team can explain which segment is prioritised and why.

Days 31-60 · Prepare

Build the first motion

Finalise messaging, sales narrative, website path, qualification criteria, CRM fields, target-account list and channel test plan.

Gate: sales and marketing use the same definition of a qualified account and meeting.

Days 61-90 · Activate

Run, measure, refine

Launch outbound and partner tests, publish proof-led content, review conversations, inspect funnel quality and update scoring and messaging.

Gate: a documented decision on what to scale, change or stop.

Operating cadence

Weekly evidence review

Track account coverage, reply quality, discovery outcomes, objections, qualified pipeline and next-step completion. Separate activity metrics from commercial outcomes.

This roadmap is a planning example, not a delivery commitment or forecast. Timing changes with access to buyers, product readiness and available sales capacity.

How the model connects

One decision changes the next.

Market selection defines the account universe. ICP work clarifies the buying group. Positioning determines the message. Channel strategy determines how that message reaches the buyer. The roadmap sequences the tests and operational work.
