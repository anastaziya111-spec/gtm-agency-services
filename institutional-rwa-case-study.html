01 / Project overview

An institutional offer in a market where trust is part of the product.

Kaizen operates in the institutional real-world assets space. The project documents describe an offer connected to RWA tokenisation and securitisation, serving capital owners and institutional intermediaries rather than a retail crypto audience.

The strategic engagement was framed around a 90-day commercial programme. Its documented scope connected market and competitor research, ICP definition, positioning, reputation management, sales assets, attribution and CRM workflows.

02 / Business challenge

The public story and the institutional offer were not aligned.

The project proposal identified three connected issues. They are presented here as the working diagnosis documented in the proposal, not as independently audited findings.

Perception

Category mismatch

The public-facing presence was described as carrying a crypto-launchpad impression while the commercial offer was institutional RWA and securitisation.

Credibility

Trust friction before the first call

Search results, review surfaces and third-party references were identified as points that could create doubt during an institutional buyer's independent research.

Commercial execution

A manual path to conversion

The proposal described a founder-led enquiry-to-deal process, limited source attribution and a long decision cycle. Exact baseline figures are intentionally not published in this preview.

Operating model

Disconnected commercial assets

Segmentation, qualification, sales materials, CRM data and follow-up needed to work as one buyer journey rather than separate activities.

The underlying proposal contains client-reported baseline figures. They are not reproduced here because no independently verified baseline or approved public disclosure was available for this page build.

03 / Market context

Institutional buyers need a credible story before they enter a sales process.

The project thesis treated market perception and third-party credibility as commercial inputs. For a high-value institutional offer, a buyer's view is shaped by more than a sales deck. Public positioning, website information, search results, references, team credentials and the clarity of the transaction process all contribute to the first assessment.

Research questions captured in the project scope

  • How the institutional RWA and securitisation category is described by comparable providers.
  • Which RWA use cases and market examples matter to the intended buyer groups.
  • Where category language, credibility signals and proof points differ across public touchpoints.
  • Which market and media environments should be prioritised for awareness and buyer education.

05 / Positioning & messaging

Make the institutional proposition unmistakable.

The positioning direction documented in the proposal was to move the public narrative away from a generic crypto-launchpad impression and toward the institutional RWA and securitisation offer.

Messaging architecture proposed in the project documents

  • Category clarity: explain the institutional RWA proposition in terms a capital owner and their advisor can assess.
  • Offer clarity: describe what the business structures, for whom, and under what process.
  • Credibility: make licences, team, audits, relevant documentation and institutional references easier to find and evaluate.
  • Process: show a clear five-step journey and give qualified prospects a useful next action.
  • Audience relevance: adapt proof, language and sales materials for principals and their advisors.
These are strategic directions from the proposal and service scope. The final approved message house and live-page copy were not present in the source set reviewed for this build.

06 / GTM strategy

A 90-day sequence from diagnosis to activation.

The documented plan connected four phases. The sequence below reflects the proposal and scope timetable. It is not presented as proof that each phase was completed.

Month 1 · Discover

Establish the baseline

Business immersion, ICP and differentiation work, competitor and RWA case research, reputation and attribution baseline, lost-prospect interviews and PR-target mapping.

Month 2 · Design

Define the commercial model

Segment sales strategies, buyer journey and qualification logic, messaging and scripts, paid institutional assessment design, and SEO / AI-search plan.

Month 2 · Build

Prepare the operating assets

Revised RWA page and qualification form, sales asset library, RWA explainer, LinkedIn presence, CRM funnel, tracking and automated intake.

Month 3 · Launch

Activate and measure

Lead generation against agreed KPIs, CRM sequences and automation, PR pilot, events plan, sales-hire support and a quarter-close report.

Channel roles in the documented plan

  • Direct sales: segmented institutional outreach and qualification.
  • Referral network: advisors and intermediaries with access to relevant capital owners.
  • Owned presence: LinkedIn and the institutional RWA page.
  • Earned visibility: finance media, analysts, events and speaking opportunities.
  • Search and trust: SEO, AI-search presence, review surfaces and public credibility signals.
  • Revenue operations: CRM source attribution, stage tracking, follow-up and reporting.

07 / Strategic recommendations

Prioritise the constraints that sit closest to revenue.

The following recommendations are derived from the documented diagnosis and proposed commercial model. They should be read as strategic recommendations, not as completed implementation.

  1. Resolve category mismatch first. Align the public story, website, sales narrative and third-party profiles around the institutional offer.
  2. Build segment-specific sales paths. Give family offices, banks, funds and referral partners distinct qualification logic, proof and next steps.
  3. Productise the first paid step. Use the Structured Institutional Assessment as a defined entry product, with scope, price, credit rules and service levels.
  4. Make credibility inspectable. Bring relevant credentials, team, process, audit and reference material into the buyer journey.
  5. Instrument the funnel before optimising it. Connect source, segment, stage and outcome in one CRM view; establish a baseline before setting improvement claims.
  6. Use feedback to change priorities. Review objections, lost-prospect reasons, channel contribution and deal progression on a fixed cadence.
